**Red-team questions + honest answers:**
- *Q: How did you confirm it was a bent pin and not another fault?* → Verified continuity at driver and Arduino ports with a multimeter, cross-checked wiring against the manufacturer's map, then observed the pin visually during an attempt to remove the Arduino from the enclosure.
- *Q: Why GRBL and not Mach3 or LinuxCNC?* → GRBL 1.1h was the factory firmware on the Satycsa 6174. Re-flashing wasn't in scope; client wanted the machine running, not rebuilt.
- *Q: Why 3D-printed brackets instead of machined replacements?* → Cost, turnaround (same-day vs. weeks), and loading on those specific parts is low — brackets are dimensional fixtures, not structural. An aluminum version is recommended for long-term production, documented in the handoff notes.
- *Q: What would you do differently?* → Install the emergency-stop wiring completion before departure. The operator damage post-handoff likely could have been prevented with a proper e-stop path and more operator training time. I documented it as pending but should have pushed to finish on-site.
